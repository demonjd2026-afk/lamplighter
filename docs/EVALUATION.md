## How to read this

- **Correct outcome** is the expected final state: RESOLVED where an automated fix exists, ESCALATED where a human must decide, and no incident or SELF_RESOLVED where nothing should be done.
- **Detect** is the time from the first failed customer request to the incident opening. **Customer impact** is the time from the fault to the last failed customer request, measured at the edge.
- **Naive responder** is scored on its decision only (it is not executed): roll back or revert the most recent change before the failure, otherwise restart the service behind the edge.

## Limits of this evaluation

- The windows are scaled down to seconds (the `demo` timing profile). Production defaults use 5 minute and 1 hour windows and a 2 minute hold, so real response times are minutes, not seconds.
- Eight scenarios on one small system. They were written alongside the diagnosis rules, so this measures that the system does what it is designed to do, not how it generalises to unseen systems.
- One fault at a time. Overlapping faults are expected to end in ESCALATED, and are not measured here.
- For the two scenarios that end in ESCALATED the fault is still present when the run stops, so no customer-impact time is reported. `restart_ineffective` escalates only after the restart fails its proof, about 40 seconds after the action in this timing profile.
